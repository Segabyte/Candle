# AI Prompt — "LifeQuest: Learn Life One Move at a Time"

> Paste everything below into an AI (Claude, GPT, an image model for the
> visual sections) to generate the game. Written to be used whole, or split —
> the *Visual Direction* section works on its own as an image prompt.

---

Design an original educational life-learning board game called **"LifeQuest"**.

Use the attached reference image only as inspiration for the general physical
board-game experience: a colorful square board, an outer travel path, cards in
the center, player tokens, dice, money/points, and clearly divided spaces. Do
not copy any existing game's artwork, names, logos, wording, exact board
layout, or copyrighted visual elements.

---

## Core Concept

LifeQuest is a board game where **every move teaches the player something
useful about life**.

Players travel through a symbolic journey of life. Every space creates a small
learning experience through:

- Questions
- Decisions
- Real-life scenarios
- Challenges
- Reflection
- Problem solving
- Mini quizzes
- Risk and reward
- Unexpected life events
- Opportunities to help another player
- Personal-growth exercises

The goal should **not** simply be to reach the finish line first.

Players should earn **Life Points** by learning, making good decisions,
overcoming challenges, helping others, and developing different areas of life.

## Game Modes

### 1. Default Life Mode

If the player does not select a topic, the game automatically uses **Life
Skills**.

Life Skills should mix topics such as:

- Money and financial literacy
- Health and wellness
- Relationships
- Communication
- Emotional intelligence
- Career
- Education
- Technology
- AI
- Critical thinking
- Decision making
- Time management
- Creativity
- Entrepreneurship
- Personal safety
- Ethics
- Environment
- Community
- Happiness
- Resilience
- Goal setting
- General knowledge
- Practical everyday skills

The player should continuously encounter different areas of life.

### 2. Choose Your Learning Journey

Before starting, allow each player to **independently** choose a topic they
want to learn.

Examples:

```
Player 1: Artificial Intelligence
Player 2: Personal Finance
Player 3: English Vocabulary
Player 4: Entrepreneurship
```

The board remains shared, but when a player lands on a learning space, the
challenge should be generated based on **that player's** chosen subject.

Include preset topics such as:

AI · Computer Science · Coding · Mathematics · Science · History · Geography ·
Finance · Investing · Entrepreneurship · Leadership · Communication · English ·
French · Health · Nutrition · Philosophy · Psychology · Relationships ·
Parenting · Career Development · General Knowledge · Life Skills

Also provide **Custom Topic**:

> "Type anything you want to learn."

For example:

- "Teach me SAP S/4HANA"
- "Teach me astronomy"
- "Teach me how to start a business."

The AI should dynamically create challenges about that topic.

## Single-Player Mode

If only one human player is playing, automatically create at least one
AI/computer opponent.

Allow:

- Human vs Computer
- Human vs 2 Computer Players
- Human vs 3 Computer Players

Computer players should have different personalities and decision-making
styles. Examples:

| Player | Style |
|---|---|
| **Alex — The Explorer** | Curious and willing to take risks. |
| **Maya — The Strategist** | Carefully evaluates choices. |
| **Leo — The Entrepreneur** | Looks for opportunities and rewards. |
| **Nova — The Scholar** | Strong at knowledge challenges. |

Computer players should actually participate: answer questions, make
decisions, gain/lose Life Points, and progress through the board.

## Multiplayer

Support approximately **2–6 players**.

Each player chooses:

- Name
- Avatar / token
- Learning topic
- Difficulty
- Optional age / learning level

Difficulty options:

- Beginner
- Intermediate
- Advanced
- Expert
- **Adaptive**

In Adaptive Mode, AI should gradually understand the player's knowledge level
and automatically make future questions easier or harder.

## Board Design

Create a square board with approximately **40–60 spaces** traveling around the
perimeter.

The visual feeling should resemble a warm, nostalgic family tabletop game
combined with a modern educational game. Use colorful but sophisticated
sections.

Possible spaces:

| Space | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Learn** | Answer a question related to your selected topic. |
| **Life Choice** | Choose between two or more realistic options. The game explains the likely consequences. |
| **Challenge** | Complete a mini challenge. |
| **Opportunity** | Take a calculated opportunity that may produce rewards. |
| **Reflection** | Answer a short question about yourself or your goals. |
| **Surprise** | Draw an unpredictable Life Event. |
| **Money** | Earn, spend, invest, save, or lose virtual money. |
| **Career** | Experience a career-related decision. |
| **Health** | Encounter a physical or mental wellness scenario. |
| **Relationship** | Solve a communication or interpersonal situation. |
| **Community** | Help another player or contribute to a shared objective. |
| **Innovation** | Solve a creative problem. |
| **Risk** | Choose whether to accept a higher-risk challenge for a larger reward. |
| **Wisdom** | Learn an interesting principle, quote, concept, or practical life lesson. |

## Every Move Must Have Meaning

Avoid meaningless turns. Whenever possible, every dice roll should cause
something educational or thought-provoking to happen.

For example:

> You rolled 5 and landed on **LEARN**.
>
> AI asks: *"Why do banks pay interest on savings accounts?"*
>
> Player answers.
>
> AI gives immediate feedback: *"Correct. Banks use deposited money as part of
> their lending operations and compensate depositors with interest."*
>
> **+2 Knowledge Points**

If incorrect, do not simply say "wrong." Explain the concept briefly so the
player still learns from the turn.

### Scenario Example

A player studying Personal Finance lands on **Life Choice**. Display:

> You received an unexpected $1,000. What would you do?
>
> A. Spend all of it
> B. Put it into savings
> C. Pay high-interest debt
> D. Invest all of it immediately

After the player chooses, explain that there may not always be one universally
correct answer. The AI should discuss the decision based on factors such as
emergency savings, interest rates, debt, goals, and risk.

**The objective is to teach reasoning, not only memorization.**

## AI Learning Engine

AI should act as the game's:

- Teacher
- Game master
- Question generator
- Storyteller
- Opponent
- Coach
- Difficulty controller

The AI should remember, within the current game:

- Questions already asked
- Topics the player understands
- Topics the player struggles with
- Player decisions
- Player progress

Avoid repeatedly asking the same question. Periodically revisit concepts that
the player previously answered incorrectly.

## Life Dimensions

Track player development across several dimensions rather than using money
alone. Create approximately six attributes:

- ❤️ **Wellbeing**
- 🧠 **Knowledge**
- 💰 **Wealth**
- 🤝 **Relationships**
- 🚀 **Growth**
- 🌍 **Contribution**

Players should see a small **Life Dashboard** showing their progress. This
reinforces the idea that success in life has multiple dimensions.

### Example Life Event Cards

- "Your phone suddenly stops working. Pay $300 to replace it or continue
  without it for two turns."
- "You helped another player understand a difficult question. Gain +2
  Contribution Points."
- "You spent several weeks learning a new skill. Gain +3 Knowledge Points."
- "You ignored your health while focusing entirely on work. Lose 2 Wellbeing
  Points."
- "You identified an opportunity others missed. Gain an Opportunity Card."
- "You created something useful for your community. Gain +3 Contribution
  Points."

Instead of traditional property ownership, players move through broad stages
or milestones such as:

```
START
  ↓
Discover
  ↓
Learn
  ↓
Explore
  ↓
Build Skills
  ↓
Career
  ↓
Relationships
  ↓
Create
  ↓
Contribute
  ↓
Wisdom
```

However, players should be free to experience these events in different orders
rather than implying that everyone must follow one fixed life path.

## Winning

Do not make winning depend only on money. At the end, calculate a **Life
Score** using several dimensions. For example:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | 20% |
| Wellbeing | 20% |
| Relationships | 15% |
| Wealth | 15% |
| Growth | 15% |
| Contribution | 15% |

Also award achievements such as:

- 🏆 Most Knowledgeable
- ❤️ Best Wellbeing
- 💡 Best Problem Solver
- 🤝 Most Helpful Player
- 🚀 Greatest Growth
- 💰 Best Financial Decisions
- 🌍 Greatest Contribution

**The overall winner is the player who builds the strongest balanced life.**

## Important Game Philosophy

The experience should feel like:

> **Board Game + AI Tutor + Life Simulator + Family Game + Personal
> Development Journey**

It should be fun enough that players want to keep playing even when they are
not consciously trying to study.

The core philosophy is:

> **PLAY → CHOOSE → THINK → LEARN → GROW**

Every turn should leave the player knowing, understanding, considering, or
practicing something they did not know before.
